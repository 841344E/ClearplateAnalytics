ClearPlate Analytics is a browser-based B2B subscription for independent Adelaide cafes. Owners connect point-of-sale data or upload a CSV, enter recipe costs and complete a short closing count. The service then recommends tomorrow's quantity with a confidence range, while the owner approves every decision. Safe unavoidable surplus can be offered to a recipient that accepts the collection window. Genuine primary research from 12 venue decision-makers shifted the concept from donation-only to prediction-first, but a three-cafe paid pilot remains the validation gate. ClearPlate seeks $48,000 for 12% ordinary equity, subject to final legal documents. At $78 weighted monthly revenue and $10 variable cost per venue, $3,315 fixed monthly costs require 49 paying venues. The base forecast reaches 62 venues in M12. Expansion depends on verified savings, paid retention, controlled digital risk and measured social and environmental outcomes.

01

The business

From a good cause to a paid daily decision

Genuine primary research. Twelve venue responses and five follow-up interviews were collected on 27 October 2026 using the 13-question instrument. Identities are anonymised. This targeted sample is decision-useful but is not representative of all Adelaide venues. Build funding still requires three signed pilot agreements.

Download anonymised primary research workbook ↓

Australia wastes about 7.6 million tonnes of food each year, costing more than $36.6 billion and producing about 3% of national greenhouse emissions.[1] ClearPlate addresses one local cause: independent cafe owners order tomorrow's food while balancing costly leftovers against lost sales. Its purpose is to make that decision measurable, then direct safe unavoidable surplus to an accepting charity.

The model evolved through evidence rather than remaining a donation app. All five interview participants required stockout protection and judged a 60-second closing task feasible when setup and defaults reduced friction. Applying this business intelligence produced three linked pivots.

Customers receive a responsive web application, not an app-store download. It works on a cafe's existing phone, tablet or computer. Onboarding connects one point-of-sale system or imports a CSV, records recipe costs and sets an owner-approved service level. Payment is collected monthly in advance by card.

ClearPlate would operate as an Adelaide proprietary limited company. The founder controls product and finance, a contracted developer maintains the platform and an onboarding contractor supports venues. Development contracts assign source-code ownership and confidentiality to ClearPlate. Trade mark registration, cyber insurance and professional indemnity quotations precede launch.

02

The market

A narrow customer, a visible transaction

The entry segment is independent Adelaide cafes and bakeries with repeatable menus, digital sales records and an owner who still controls ordering. Spreadsheets cost little and MarketMan already forecasts demand, so ClearPlate cannot claim a unique algorithm.[2] Square also connects venues to ingredient inventory through MarketMan.[3] The proposed advantage is a smaller daily task, transparent confidence ranges and recipient-confirmed surplus coordination.

A free 14-day measurement audit demonstrates the hidden cost before requesting payment. The year-one funnel assumes 400 qualified prospects, 200 demonstrations, 100 trials, 70 activations and eight exits, leaving 62 retained venues. Roaster referrals and direct precinct demonstrations diversify acquisition. If fewer than 50% of trials activate, advertising pauses while onboarding is revised.

03

The strategy

Validation gates control the risk

The eight-week pilot uses two baseline weeks and six intervention weeks across three cafes. Results are normalised per $100 of comparable sales. Growth proceeds only if ingredient cost falls at least 8%, stockout events do not rise, median daily input stays below two minutes, forecast error beats a same-weekday baseline by 10% and at least two venues authorise paid Growth subscriptions. Failure retains the measurement tool, changes the forecast or stops the build.

Digital opportunity and exposure

Cloud delivery makes recurring national access possible, but AE2 requires the downside to be designed into operations. The platform stores business contacts and venue-level sales totals only; POS imports exclude customer names and identifiers. A payment processor holds card details. Most businesses below $3 million turnover are generally exempt from the Privacy Act unless an exception applies, yet ClearPlate will voluntarily use an Australian Privacy Principles approach because legal minimums do not equal customer trust.[4] ASD reports an average self-reported cybercrime cost of $56,571 for small businesses in 2024-25, which could exhaust this venture's capital.[5]

Measured environmental and social value

AE3 is measured without double-counting. The pilot weighs edible food discarded, offered, accepted and collected. Targets are 15% less edible discard per $100 sales, at least 80% of accepted collections completed and zero unsafe handovers. OzHarvest's 2025 results imply about two meals and one kilogram of CO2-e avoided per kilogram rescued, but ClearPlate will use that only as an external benchmark, not claim OzHarvest outcomes or partnership.[6]

Donation follows prevention so social reporting never rewards overproduction. Recipient acceptance comes before pickup. Time, temperature, allergen and weight records support safe handover under food standards.[7] South Australia's donor protection in section 74A does not protect knowingly or recklessly unsafe supply.[8] Unaccepted food moves to an approved organics stream.

04

The finance

Every number reaches the investor decision

ClearPlate asks Adelaide early-stage investors for $48,000 in exchange for 12% ordinary equity, subject to final legal documents. This implies a $400,000 post-investment valuation, a $352,000 pre-investment valuation and 88% founder ownership. Allocate $22,000 to the web build, $5,000 to the measured pilot and $21,000 to operating liquidity. Development payments are milestone-based. Failed pilot gates stop unreleased spending.

At 49 venues, monthly revenue is $3,822 and costs are $3,805, leaving $17. At 62 venues, revenue is $4,836 and costs are $3,935, leaving $901. Budgeted CAC payback is $150 ÷ $68 = 2.21 months. If only 31 venues remain, CAC doubles to $300 and payback extends to 4.41 months, so conversion quality matters more than web traffic.

The forecast assumes no subscription revenue in M1-3, monthly billing from M4 and no founder drawings in year one. It reaches operating break-even in M10 and 62 venues in M12. The investment case is therefore conditional: prove customer savings, paid retention, safe impact and cash discipline before a second region.

PITCH TRANSCRIPT

Independent cafes choose tomorrow's food before demand is known. Too much becomes cash in the bin. Too little loses sales. ClearPlate makes that decision measurable.

Twelve genuine responses changed the model. Eight ranked over-ordering in their top two, none ranked donation logistics there and seven accepted a trial. Prediction became the paid core.

ClearPlate is a responsive website, not a downloadable app. Sales, recipes and closing counts produce tomorrow's quantity and confidence range. The owner approves or overrides it, then a stockout flag improves the next forecast.

Customers pay monthly by card. Sprout measures waste, Growth adds prediction and Harvest adds recipient coordination. Competitors already forecast demand, so ClearPlate's edge is a simpler daily workflow.

ClearPlate evolved from donation-only to prediction-first, then added closing counts and accepted pickups. A three-cafe pilot must cut ingredient cost eight per cent without more stockouts and convert two venues.

Each venue contributes sixty-eight dollars after variable cost. Fixed costs are three thousand three hundred and fifteen, so break-even is forty-nine venues. The base forecast reaches it in month ten.

Digital risk is budgeted: no diner identities, card details stay with a processor, cached briefs cover outages and maintenance is capped at twenty-five hundred monthly. The pilot weighs waste, collections and safety.

Adelaide investors, we seek forty-eight thousand dollars for twelve per cent ordinary equity. Twenty-two thousand builds the platform, five thousand funds the pilot and twenty-one thousand protects liquidity. Back the Adelaide proof.
